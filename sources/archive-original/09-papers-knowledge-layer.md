# The knowledge layer: what the research literature says (ontologies, retrieval, graph retrieval, structured QA, entity resolution, document parsing, numeric reasoning)

Scope note (written 21 November 2026). This file reviews the primary research literature behind the "knowledge layer" of a company-model product: the part that holds business objects, metric definitions and links between records, and that agents query to answer questions. Each paper was opened on arXiv (abstract page and, for the important ones, the full HTML version) or on the publisher or standards body site. Every figure below comes from the paper or page cited next to it unless it is labelled [Unverified] or [Inference]. Where the abstract and the body of a paper disagree, both are shown. Papers are grouped by topic; each entry gives citation, problem, method (detailed enough to implement), results, limitations and the lesson for a builder. The final sections turn this into a build recommendation for (a) questions about numbers, (b) questions answered from documents, and (c) multi-hop questions across records. Label key: [Fact] = stated in the cited source; [Vendor claim] = stated by a party selling the technique; [Inference] = my reasoning; [Unverified] = I could not confirm it from a page I opened.

## Key findings

1. **A governed semantic layer beats raw text-to-SQL on business numbers, by a large margin, in every controlled study found.** Sequeda et al. (2023): GPT-4 zero-shot over SQL scored 16.7%; over an OWL ontology plus R2RML mappings, 54.2% ([2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07509)). Adding an ontology-based query check plus LLM repair raised that to 72%, with 8% "I don't know" and a 20% error rate ([2405.11706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706)). dbt Labs' 2026 rerun on the same insurance data: Semantic Layer 98.2–100% against 84–90% for text-to-SQL on modelled data ([dbt blog, 7 Apr 2026](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026)). A 2026 paired test with three frontier models found that a 4 KB hand-written semantics document added 17–23 points (about 46–51% up to about 68%) ([2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)).
2. **The semantic layer's main advantage is how it fails, more than its accuracy.** dbt reports that text-to-SQL can return wrong numbers that look plausible, while the Semantic Layer returns an error when a question is out of scope ([dbt 2026](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026)). OBQC turns some wrong answers into "I don't know" ([2405.11706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706)). For a product that acts on behalf of staff, an explicit refusal matters more than a few accuracy points. [Inference]
3. **Schema size and normalisation drive text-to-SQL failure.** On questions that touch more than 4 normalised tables, GPT-4 text-to-SQL scored 0% in Sequeda's benchmark, against 35.7–38.5% over the knowledge graph ([2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07509)). On Spider 2.0 (enterprise databases, many with over 1,000 columns), o1-preview solved 21.3%, compared with 91.2% on Spider 1.0 and 73.0% on BIRD ([2411.07763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763)). As of November 2026, agent systems reach about 96.7 on Spider 2.0-Snow and 76.2 on Spider 2.0-Lite ([leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)). BIRD test execution accuracy tops out at 83.40 against a human score of 92.96 ([BIRD leaderboard](https://bird-bench.github.io/)).
4. **In production, metadata matters more than model choice.** LinkedIn's SQL Bot builds a knowledge graph of tables, columns, query-log statistics, example queries and certification status. With table schemas alone, only 9% of answers scored 4 or higher out of 5; with the full system, 48%. Example queries and table clustering gave most of the gain. Some ideas from the literature (multiple candidate generation, query planning) did not help ([2507.14372](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14372v1)).
5. **GraphRAG is not a general improvement on vector RAG.** On single-hop questions plain RAG wins (NQ F1 64.8 against 63.0 for the best GraphRAG variant). On multi-hop questions graph methods win by a few points (HotpotQA: HippoRAG 2 at 63.0 against RAG at 60.0). KG-only GraphRAG built from triples is much worse (NQ 34.3), and only about 65% of answer entities make it into the LLM-extracted graph ([2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). GraphRAG-Bench reaches the same conclusion: use simple RAG for fact lookup and graphs for complex reasoning and summarisation ([2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)).
6. **Graph methods differ in cost by orders of magnitude.** Average prompt tokens per query: vanilla RAG about 900, HippoRAG 2 about 1,000, MS-GraphRAG local about 39k, LightRAG about 100k, MS-GraphRAG global about 331k ([2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)). LazyGraphRAG cuts indexing cost to about 0.1% of GraphRAG's by deferring LLM work until query time ([MSR blog, Nov 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)).
7. **HippoRAG 2 is the best documented graph retriever that does not lose on simple questions.** It runs Personalized PageRank over an OpenIE phrase graph with passage nodes. Average F1 is 59.8, against 57.0 for the strong dense retriever NV-Embed-v2, and it equals or beats dense retrieval on NQ and PopQA. In the same table LightRAG averaged 6.6 ([2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802), ICML 2025).
8. **The cheapest large gain in document retrieval is contextual chunk enrichment plus hybrid search plus reranking.** On Anthropic's tests the top-20 retrieval failure rate fell from 5.7% to 1.9% (a 67% reduction), at a one-off cost of about $1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching. Below about 200k tokens, Anthropic advises skipping RAG and putting the whole corpus in the prompt ([Anthropic, 19 Sep 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Semantic chunking was not worth its cost in a controlled study ([2410.13070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070)).
9. **Long context against retrieval: route, don't choose.** When resources allow, long context beats RAG. Self-Route tries RAG first and falls back to the full context only when the model says "unanswerable". It matched long-context quality at 39–65% lower cost ([2407.16833](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16833), EMNLP 2024 industry track). LaRA (2025) finds no universal winner ([2502.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977)).
10. **LLMs can draft ontologies and schema mappings but cannot be trusted to finalise them.** In LLMs4OL, zero-shot GPT-4 reached 78.1% F1 on taxonomy discovery on UMLS but only 41.3% on non-taxonomic relations. The authors call foundation LLMs "not sufficiently suitable" without fine-tuning ([2307.16648](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16648)). In the 2024 challenge, non-taxonomic relation extraction peaked at F1 0.078 ([2409.10146](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10146v1)). Ontology drafts from o1-preview were judged usable by ontology engineers but still contained common mistakes ([2503.05388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05388v1)). Schema-to-ontology mapping works best when broken into symbolically constrained sub-tasks, about +25 F1 on RODI ([2609.13776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13776)).
11. **For extracting invoice and receipt fields, give frontier VLMs the image, not parsed markdown.** On scanned receipts, Gemini 2.5 Pro scored 87.5% field accuracy from the image against 47.0% at best after Docling conversion to markdown ([2509.04469](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04469v1)). On general PDF parsing, pipeline tools such as MinerU still lead on common layouts, while VLMs handle unusual layouts and poor scans better ([2412.07626](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07626)).
12. **Arithmetic should be done by executed code, not by the model's own reasoning.** Program-of-Thoughts gains about 12% over chain-of-thought on average, including on FinQA, ConvFinQA and TAT-QA ([2211.12588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588), TMLR 2023). On FinanceBench, GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval answered 81% of questions wrongly or refused ([2311.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)).
13. **Entity resolution is mature without LLMs.** Fellegi-Sunter scores use log2(m/u) weights (as in [Splink](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html)), and blocking keeps the number of compared pairs manageable. LLMs add robustness on hard pairs and unseen entities with few or no labels ([2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244)). Ditto reached 96.5% F1 matching company tables of 789K and 412K records ([2004.00584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00584)).
14. **LLM-as-judge evaluation of GraphRAG is badly biased by answer order.** With GPT-4o as judge, GraphRAG-Local won 97% of comparisons when shown first and 12% when shown second ([2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). Many headline GraphRAG and LightRAG "win rates" come from exactly this kind of pairwise LLM judging ([2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16130), [2410.05779](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05779)). Treat such numbers with caution and score your own evaluations in both orders.

## Breakdown

### 1. Ontology foundations

**Plain English.** An ontology is an agreed, machine-readable dictionary of the things a business talks about, how they relate, and which rules hold. Example: "a Customer places Orders; an Order has one or more OrderLines; each OrderLine refers to exactly one Product; Net Revenue = sum of OrderLine amount minus refunds." Agents that read this dictionary don't have to guess what the column `amt_2` means.

**Gruber 1993.** Thomas R. Gruber, "A Translation Approach to Portable Ontology Specifications", *Knowledge Acquisition* 5(2):199–220, 1993 ([author page](https://tomgruber.org/writing/ontolingua-kaj-1993/)). [Fact] The paper is the source of the definition "a specification of a conceptualization". Problem: AI systems could not share knowledge because each used its own representation. Method: write ontologies once in a standard predicate-calculus form, then use Ontolingua to translate them into the target systems (frame systems, relational languages). A "Frame Ontology" bridges object-oriented and relational representations. Hard parts: keeping meaning intact across systems with different styles, and translating from expressive to restricted languages. Builder lesson: [Inference] keep the company model in one canonical, declarative form and *compile* it into each runtime target (SQL views, a graph store, tool schemas for agents, prompt context). Don't let each target drift away from the canonical form.

**Guarino 1998.** N. Guarino, "Formal Ontology and Information Systems", in *Formal Ontology in Information Systems: Proceedings of FOIS'98*, Trento, 6–8 June 1998, IOS Press, pp. 3–15 ([PDF](https://www.loa.istc.cnr.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/FOIS98.pdf)). [Fact] Defines an ontology as a logical theory accounting for the intended meaning of a formal vocabulary. A conceptualisation is language-independent; an ontology only approximates the "intended models" and usually also admits unintended ones. Ontologies are layered: top-level, domain, task, and application (domain plus task, often modelling roles). Guarino separates *ontology-aware* systems (which can consult an ontology) from *ontology-driven* ones (where the ontology is an active component), at both development and run time. At run time he lists database mediation, intensional queries, user-interface vocabulary mapping and agent communication. Builder lesson: [Inference] the product should be ontology-driven at run time. Agents' tools, permissions and query compilation should be *generated from* the model rather than written by hand next to it. Layer the model: a small fixed top level (Party, Document, Event, Amount, Period), domain packs per industry, and an application layer per customer.

**OntoClean (Guarino & Welty).** "Evaluating Ontological Decisions with OntoClean", *Communications of the ACM*, 2002 ([CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/evaluating-ontological-decisions-with-ontoclean); volume and issue listed by [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/GuarinoW02) as 45(2) [Unverified: page numbers]). [Fact] Meta-properties: rigidity (rigid, semi-rigid, anti-rigid), identity and unity. Key constraint: an anti-rigid property cannot subsume a rigid one. Example: Student cannot be a superclass of Person. Builder lesson: [Inference] model *roles* (Customer, Supplier, Employee) as relationships or role objects attached to rigid kinds (Person, Organisation), not as subclasses. The same company can be both a customer and a supplier. An LLM-generated ontology will often get this wrong, so add a lint rule for it.

**RDFS, OWL 2 and SHACL in brief.**
- RDF models data as subject–predicate–object triples with global IRIs. RDFS adds classes, subclass, domain and range. [Fact] RDF 1.2 is a Candidate Recommendation Draft dated 16 Nov 2026. It adds *triple terms*: a triple can be the object of another triple through `rdf:reifies`, so you can record "Alice claims X" without asserting X ([W3C RDF 1.2 Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/)). This is the RDF answer to provenance and edge properties.
- OWL 2 profiles (W3C Recommendation, 11 Dec 2012) ([OWL 2 Profiles](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/)): [Fact] **EL** has polynomial-time reasoning and suits very large class hierarchies such as SNOMED CT. **QL** answers queries in AC0 data complexity by *rewriting queries into SQL over the unchanged relational data*, the basis of "virtual knowledge graphs". **RL** is implementable with rule engines and has polynomial data complexity. Builder lesson: [Inference] OWL 2 QL is the closest formal match to "an ontology over the customer's existing databases without copying data". Its restrictions (no transitive properties, no cardinality, limited existentials) are a useful upper bound on what the model should express if queries are to compile to SQL.
- SHACL (W3C Recommendation, 20 July 2017) ([SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/)): [Fact] a *shapes graph* validates a *data graph*. Node shapes and property shapes with `sh:path` are selected by targets (`sh:targetClass` and others). Core constraints include `sh:minCount`/`sh:maxCount`, `sh:datatype`, `sh:class`, `sh:pattern`, `sh:in` and `sh:closed`. Output is a `sh:ValidationReport` with `sh:conforms` and one result per failure (focus node, path, value, severity, message). SHACL-SPARQL adds custom constraints. [Inference, also the page's own reading] SHACL is closed-world: missing data is a violation. OWL is open-world: missing data is just unknown. Business rules ("every invoice must have exactly one supplier") are SHACL-style, not OWL-style.

**RDF compared with labelled property graphs (LPG), and ISO GQL.** [Fact] ISO/IEC 39075:2024, "GQL", edition 1, April 2024, 610 pages, from JTC 1/SC 32. It defines property-graph data structures and a language to create, query, maintain and control them; a corrigendum came out in 2026 and a revision is under development ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/76120.html)). [Inference] The practical differences: an LPG stores key–value properties directly on nodes *and edges* (for example the date and amount on an "ordered" edge) and has a now-standard query language. RDF has global identifiers, a formal-semantics stack (RDFS/OWL), validation (SHACL), and, with RDF 1.2, statement-level annotation. For a small product neither is required as a storage engine. The company model can live in relational tables (object types, link types, properties), with a GQL/Cypher-style or SPARQL-style *view* generated where traversal is needed. Sequeda's results (section 5) come from the *semantic context* the ontology adds, not from the RDF storage format.

### 2. Knowledge graphs and LLMs: roadmaps and surveys

**Pan et al. 2023/2024.** S. Pan, L. Luo, Y. Wang, C. Chen, J. Wang, X. Wu, "Unifying Large Language Models and Knowledge Graphs: A Roadmap", arXiv:2306.08302, *IEEE TKDE* (DOI 10.1109/TKDE.2024.3352100) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)). [Fact] Three frameworks: (1) KG-enhanced LLMs, where KGs are used in pre-training, inference and interpretability; (2) LLM-augmented KGs, where LLMs help with KG embedding, completion, construction, graph-to-text and KGQA; (3) synergised LLM+KG with two-way reasoning. Builder lesson: [Inference] a product that uses third-party models without training them works only in framework 1 *at inference time* and framework 2 *for construction*. The training-based parts of the roadmap (pre-training with KGs, KG-tuned models) are off the table.

**Newer surveys (2024–2026).**
- Peng et al., "Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey", arXiv:2408.08921 (Aug 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)). [Fact] Divides GraphRAG into graph-based indexing, graph-guided retrieval and graph-enhanced generation. Useful as a checklist of design slots.
- Ma et al., "Large Language Models Meet Knowledge Graphs for Question Answering: Synthesis and Opportunities", arXiv:2505.20099 (May 2025, v2 Sep 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20099v2)). [Fact] Classifies methods by complex-QA task type and by the role the KG plays.
- Bian, "LLM-empowered knowledge graph construction: A survey", arXiv:2510.20345 (Oct 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20345)). [Fact] Covers ontology engineering, extraction and fusion, and contrasts schema-based approaches (normalisation, consistency) with schema-free ones (open discovery).
- Jelodar et al., "Integrating Graphs, Large Language Models, and Agents: Reasoning and Retrieval", arXiv:2604.15951 (Apr 2026) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15951)). [Fact] Classifies by purpose, graph modality and integration strategy (prompting, augmentation, training, agents).
- Singh et al., "Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey on Agentic RAG", arXiv:2501.09136 (Jan 2025, v4 Apr 2026) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09136)). [Fact] The agentic patterns it names are reflection, planning, tool use and multi-agent collaboration.
Builder lesson: [Inference] the surveys are for orientation only and report no new head-to-head numbers. For decisions, rely on the controlled comparisons in sections 4 and 5.

### 3. Learning ontologies and schemas with LLMs

**LLMs4OL (2023).** H. Babaei Giglou, J. D'Souza, S. Auer, arXiv:2307.16648 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16648)). Problem: can LLMs do ontology learning? Method: zero-shot prompting of nine model families on three tasks: (A) term typing, (B) taxonomy discovery (is-a), (C) non-taxonomic relations. Sources were WordNet, GeoNames and UMLS (plus schema.org for B). Results [Fact]:
- Task A, MAP@1: WordNet 91.7% (GPT-3.5); GeoNames 43.3% (GPT-4, the text says 39.4%); UMLS-NCI 16.1%.
- Task B, F1: UMLS 78.1% (GPT-4), schema.org 74.4%, GeoNames 67.8%.
- Task C, F1: best 49.5% (Flan-T5-XL), with GPT-4 at 41.3%.
- Fine-tuning Flan-T5-XL raised Task A on WordNet from 52.2% to 86.3%.
Conclusion: foundation LLMs are not sufficient for ontology construction on their own, though fine-tuned ones may serve as assistants.

**LLMs4OL 2024 Challenge (ISWC 2024).** arXiv:2409.10146 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10146v1)). [Fact] 272 submissions from 14 participants. Best F1: term typing on WordNet 0.994, but GeoNames 0.59 and Gene Ontology about 0.27–0.30. Taxonomy discovery on Gene Ontology 0.049. Non-taxonomic relations on UMLS 0.078. Fine-tuning beat RAG, and performance fell from few-shot to zero-shot. Builder lesson: [Inference] LLMs are good at *naming and typing* things and weak at *proposing relationships*. In a company model, relationships should come from foreign keys, join statistics in query logs and human confirmation, not from free LLM generation.

**SPIRES / OntoGPT.** Caufield et al., "Structured prompt interrogation and recursive extraction of semantics (SPIRES)", arXiv:2304.02711, *Bioinformatics* (DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btae104) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02711)); code at [OntoGPT](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/ontogpt). Method [Fact]: the user supplies a schema; the LLM is prompted recursively to fill each schema slot, nested objects trigger further prompts, and matched values are grounded to identifiers from existing ontologies and vocabularies, zero-shot. Accuracy is described as comparable to the mid-range of relation-extraction methods. OntoGPT works with many providers through LiteLLM; the CLI is `ontogpt extract -i file -t template`. The schema language (LinkML) is suggested only by the repository's topic tags; the README I opened does not explain it [Unverified detail]. Builder lesson: [Inference] SPIRES is the right pattern for *populating* the company model from documents (contracts, policies): extract against the typed object schema, then ground each value to an existing object ID through entity resolution. Never let extraction mint free-text entities.

**Ontology generation from requirements.** Lippolis et al., "Ontology Generation using Large Language Models", arXiv:2503.05388 (Mar 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05388v1)). [Fact] Two prompting methods, Memoryless CQbyCQ (one competency question at a time) and Ontogenia. Benchmark: 10 ontologies, 100 competency questions, 29 user stories. o1-preview with Ontogenia produced drafts good enough for ontology engineers and better than novice engineers, but with recurring mistakes and variable quality. Builder lesson: [Inference] drive onboarding with *competency questions* ("What was net revenue by outlet last month?"). Generate the model one question at a time, and keep each question as a regression test.

**Ontologies from relational databases.**
- RIGOR: Nayyeri et al., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Ontologies from Relational Databases", arXiv:2506.01232 (Jun 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01232v1)). [Fact] Works table by table, following foreign keys. For each step it retrieves from (i) the schema and its documentation, (ii) a repository of domain ontologies, and (iii) the growing core ontology. The LLM emits *provenance-tagged delta fragments*; a judge LLM refines each before it is merged. The abstract gives no numbers.
- Thombre, Patwardhan, Sarawagi, "Surprising Effectiveness of Self-Demonstrations in Enhancing Schema-Ontology Mapping with LLMs", arXiv:2609.13776 (Sep 2026) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13776)). [Fact] Splits mapping into cascaded sub-tasks in which symbolic constraints narrow the candidates and the LLM reasons only inside each sub-task, guided by self-generated demonstrations built from domain-agnostic patterns. Reports about +25 F1 over traditional and LLM baselines on three hard RODI scenarios. One-shot prompting and naive multi-stage pipelines did badly.
- Zhang & Zhu (Yonyou AI Lab), "Construct, Align, and Reason: Large Ontology Models for Enterprise Knowledge Management", arXiv:2602.00029 (Jan 2026) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00029v1)). [Fact] Finds implicit foreign keys in legacy databases with a multi-factor confidence score (name similarity, type compatibility, value overlap, cardinality). Extracts triples from text with Qwen3, disambiguates with BGE-M3 embeddings, and fuses the two. Requires training a graph-encoder plus Qwen3-4B model, evaluated on 190 synthetic graph-algorithm tasks. The construction pipeline itself has no quantitative evaluation. Builder lesson: copy the *implicit-FK scoring* (useful on messy SME databases); ignore the trained model.

**Ontology matching.** Qiang, Wang, Taylor, "Agent-OM: Leveraging LLM Agents for Ontology Matching", arXiv:2312.00326 (v29, Oct 2026) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00326)). [Fact] Two Siamese agents (retrieval and matching) with matching tools, evaluated on three OAEI tracks. Close to the long-standing best on simple tasks; clearly better on complex and few-shot tasks. Builder lesson: [Inference] use this pattern to map a customer's schema onto the product's domain-pack ontology. Candidate generation by embedding retrieval, then LLM judgement per candidate pair, then human confirmation.

### 4. Retrieval over text

**RAG (Lewis et al. 2020).** "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks", arXiv:2005.11401 (v4, Apr 2021) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)). [Fact] A pre-trained seq2seq generator (parametric memory) is paired with a dense vector index of Wikipedia (non-parametric memory) accessed through a neural retriever. Two variants: the same passages for the whole output, or different passages per token. State of the art on three open-domain QA tasks at the time. The abstract page does not name DPR or BART, the NeurIPS 2020 venue, or the exact-match scores; I recall all three but did not confirm them on a page [Unverified]. Builder lesson: the base pattern; everything below refines it.

**Dense, sparse and hybrid retrieval; reranking.** BEIR: Thakur et al., arXiv:2104.08663, NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663)). [Fact] 18 datasets, 10 systems. BM25 is a robust zero-shot baseline; dense retrievers often generalise worse out of domain; rerankers and late-interaction models score best but cost the most compute. Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval post (19 Sep 2024) ([post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)) gives the most implementable recipe found:
- For each chunk, prompt a cheap model (Claude 3 Haiku) with the *whole document* plus the chunk, asking for a 50–100-token situating context.
- Prepend that context to the chunk, then build both the embedding index and a BM25 index.
- Retrieve the top 150, rerank, and keep the top 20.
Failure rate (top-20 recall miss): 5.7% baseline, 3.7% with contextual embeddings, 2.9% with contextual BM25 added, 1.9% with reranking. Cost about $1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching (assuming 800-token chunks and 8k-token documents). [Vendor claim, since Anthropic sells the model used] Below 200k tokens, put the whole knowledge base in the prompt.

**Chunking.**
- Qu, Tu, Bao, "Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?", arXiv:2410.13070 (Oct 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070)). [Fact] Across document retrieval, evidence retrieval and answer generation, semantic chunking's cost "not justified by consistent performance gains" over fixed-size chunks.
- Jimeno Yepes et al., "Financial Report Chunking for Effective RAG", arXiv:2402.05131 (Feb 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05131)). [Fact] Chunks by structural element (title, table, list item) detected by document-understanding models, removing the need to tune chunk size; improves RAG on financial reports. The headline numbers are in the full PDF, which I did not open [Unverified numbers].
- Late chunking: Günther et al. (Jina AI), arXiv:2409.04701 (v3, Jul 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04701)). [Fact] Embed the whole long document with a long-context embedding model, then mean-pool token embeddings per chunk span *after* the transformer, so each chunk vector carries document context. No training needed. The abstract gives no figures.
Builder lesson: [Inference] use structural chunks (sections, table blocks) plus contextual prefixes. Late chunking is an alternative to LLM-written prefixes when the embedding model supports long inputs and LLM cost matters.

**Late interaction (ColBERT).** Khattab & Zaharia, arXiv:2004.12832 (2020) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)). [Fact] Query and document are encoded independently into per-token vectors; a cheap interaction step scores them; document vectors are precomputed. About 100× faster and about 10,000× fewer FLOPs per query than BERT cross-encoder rerankers, at competitive quality. ColBERTv2, arXiv:2112.01488 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)), adds residual compression that shrinks the index 6–10×. The scoring operator (MaxSim: for each query token, take the maximum similarity over document tokens, then sum) is from my knowledge of the paper, not the abstract [Unverified on page]. Builder lesson: [Inference] a reranker on top of hybrid retrieval gets most of the benefit with less infrastructure. Consider late interaction only when reranking latency becomes the bottleneck.

**RAPTOR.** Sarthi et al. (Stanford), arXiv:2401.18059 (Jan 2024) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.18059)). Method [Fact]:
- Split into 100-token chunks (sentences kept whole) and embed with SBERT multi-qa-mpnet.
- Soft-cluster with a Gaussian mixture model after UMAP; choose the number of clusters by BIC; cluster globally, then locally; re-cluster clusters that are too large.
- Summarise each cluster with gpt-3.5-turbo and repeat up the tree.
- Query a "collapsed tree" (all layers at once) with a 2,000-token budget. This beat tree traversal.
Results: QuALITY 82.6% with GPT-4 (prior best 62.3%); QASPER F1 55.7; about 4% of summary nodes had minor hallucinations; build cost grew linearly with document length. Limits: summaries are lossy and the GMM assumption fits text poorly. In HippoRAG 2's comparison RAPTOR trailed dense retrieval on average (48.8 against 57.0 F1) ([2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802)). Builder lesson: [Inference] useful for long single documents (a 200-page contract, a policy manual) when broad "what does this say about X" questions are expected. Not a default.

**Self-RAG.** Asai et al., arXiv:2310.11511 (Oct 2023) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11511)). [Fact] The model emits reflection tokens: Retrieve (yes/no/continue), IsRel, IsSup (fully, partially, or no support) and IsUse (1–5). A critic (Llama 2-7B) is trained on 4k–20k GPT-4-labelled examples per token type, with over 90% agreement with GPT-4. Inference uses segment-level beam search scored by a weighted sum of token probabilities. *It requires training the generator*, so it cannot be used with closed third-party models. Builder lesson: [Inference] copy the *checks*, not the training. After generation, ask the model (or a cheaper one) whether each claim is fully supported by a cited passage, and block unsupported claims.

**Corrective RAG (CRAG).** Yan et al., arXiv:2401.15884 (v3 Oct 2024) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15884)). Method [Fact]:
- A fine-tuned T5-large evaluator (0.77B parameters) scores each of about 10 retrieved documents from −1 to 1.
- Per-dataset thresholds decide the action: Correct if any document exceeds the upper threshold, Incorrect if all fall below the lower one, Ambiguous otherwise. Example: PopQA uses (0.59, −0.99).
- Correct: split documents into strips, score each strip, and keep the top 5 above −0.5.
- Incorrect: rewrite the question into at most 3 keywords and run a web search (top 5 URLs).
- Ambiguous: combine both.
Gains over standard RAG with SelfRAG-LLaMA2-7b: PopQA +7.0, Biography FactScore +14.9, PubHealth +36.6, Arc +15.4. Limit: needs a separately trained evaluator. Builder lesson: [Inference] build a *retrieval sufficiency gate*. An LLM judges whether the retrieved evidence is sufficient; if not, widen the search (other sources, structured lookup) or say "I don't know" rather than answering from weak context. In a company setting, "web search" becomes "query another internal source".

**Long context against retrieval.**
- Li et al., "Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach", arXiv:2407.16833, EMNLP 2024 industry track ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16833)). [Fact] Average scores, long context against RAG: Gemini-1.5-Pro 49.7 against 37.3; GPT-4o 48.7 against 32.6. Self-Route (RAG first; the model may answer "unanswerable", in which case retry with full context) scored 46.4 and 48.9. About 23–43% of queries were routed to long context; cost fell 65% (Gemini) and 39% (GPT-4o). RAG failure classes: multi-step queries, vague general queries, long complex queries, and implicit whole-text queries.
- Lee et al. (Google DeepMind), "Can Long-Context Language Models Subsume Retrieval, RAG, SQL, and More?" (LOFT), arXiv:2406.13121 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13121)). [Fact] Long-context models rival retrieval and RAG systems but *struggle with SQL-like compositional reasoning*.
- Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", arXiv:2307.03172, TACL 2023 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)). [Fact] Accuracy is highest when the relevant information sits at the start or end of the context and falls when it is in the middle.
- LaRA, arXiv:2502.09977 (Feb 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977)). [Fact] 2,326 cases, 11 models; the better choice depends on model size, context length, task type and chunk characteristics.
Builder lesson: [Inference] for one customer's policy handbook (often under 200k tokens), long context with caching is simpler and better. Aggregations over records should never go to long context; they go to the query engine (LOFT).

### 5. Graph retrieval

**GraphRAG (Microsoft).** Edge et al., "From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization", arXiv:2404.16130 (v2 Feb 2025) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16130)). Method [Fact]:
- Split into 600-token chunks with 100-token overlap.
- GPT-4-turbo extracts entities, relationships and claims using domain few-shot examples, with "gleaning" rounds that ask what was missed. Entities are matched by exact string; duplicate relationships become edge weights.
- Run hierarchical Leiden community detection (graspologic) at levels C0–C3.
- Summarise each community bottom-up into an 8k-token window, prioritised by prominence.
- At query time, shuffle and chunk the community summaries; each chunk yields a partial answer with a 0–100 helpfulness score; drop zeros; reduce the rest in score order.
Data: podcast transcripts (about 1M tokens, 8,564 nodes) and news (about 1.7M tokens). 125 generated questions per dataset, judged pairwise by an LLM. Results: 62–83% win rates over vector RAG on comprehensiveness and diversity; root-level summaries used about 97% fewer tokens than summarising the source text. Indexing the podcast corpus took about 281 minutes. Limits: two corpora only; fabrication not measured; designed for *global sensemaking*, not fact lookup.

**LazyGraphRAG and DRIFT.**
- LazyGraphRAG ([MSR blog, 25 Nov 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)), [Vendor claim]:
  - Indexing: NLP noun-phrase extraction, a concept co-occurrence graph and graph-statistics communities; no LLM summarisation at index time.
  - Query: an LLM splits the query into subqueries; chunks are ranked by embedding similarity and communities by their best chunks; an LLM tests chunk relevance within a fixed "relevance test budget" (100, 500 or 1,500), recursing into subcommunities when successive communities yield nothing relevant; answers are built from extracted claims.
  - Results: indexing at about 0.1% of GraphRAG's cost; quality comparable to GraphRAG global search at more than 700× lower query cost. Tested on 5,590 AP articles and 100 synthetic queries. Integrated into Microsoft Discovery per the June 2025 editor's note.
- DRIFT ([MSR blog, 31 Oct 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-drift-search-combining-global-and-local-search-methods-to-improve-quality-and-efficiency/)), [Vendor claim]: HyDE-expand the query; match it against community reports; the top-K reports produce an initial answer plus follow-up questions; run each follow-up through local search; stop after 2 iterations. Beat local search 78% (comprehensiveness) and 81% (diversity) on 50 questions, LLM-judged.

**LightRAG.** Guo et al. (HKU), arXiv:2410.05779 (v3 Apr 2025) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05779)). Method [Fact]:
- 1,200-token chunks; the LLM extracts entities and relations.
- An LLM writes a key–value profile per entity (key = name) and per relation (several keys, including themes); duplicates are merged; gleaning = 1.
- At query time the LLM extracts low-level and high-level keywords. Low-level keywords are matched to entities and high-level ones to relations in a vector database, then expanded by one hop.
- Incremental update by taking the union of node and edge sets, with no rebuild.
Results: LLM-judged win rates (about 85% over naive RAG on Legal; about 49–55% against GraphRAG). Retrieval uses fewer than 100 tokens of keyword generation in one call, against about 610k tokens for GraphRAG global on Legal. *Independent tests are much less favourable*: 6.6 average F1 in the HippoRAG 2 benchmark ([2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802)) and about 100k tokens per query in GraphRAG-Bench ([2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)). The two sources disagree sharply. [Inference] The disagreement probably reflects different metrics (LLM-judged comprehensiveness against exact-answer F1) and configurations, so it is a warning sign either way.

**HippoRAG and HippoRAG 2.** HippoRAG: Gutiérrez et al., arXiv:2405.14831, NeurIPS 2024 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831)). [Fact] LLM + KG + Personalized PageRank (PPR); up to 20% better on multi-hop QA; comparable to iterative IRCoT at 10–30× lower cost and 6–13× faster.
HippoRAG 2: "From RAG to Memory: Non-Parametric Continual Learning for LLMs", arXiv:2502.14802, ICML 2025 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802)).
- Offline [Fact]: an LLM runs OpenIE on each passage to get schema-less triples. Subjects and objects become phrase nodes and relations become edges. Synonym edges link phrase pairs whose embedding similarity exceeds a threshold. Each passage is a node linked by "contains" edges to its phrases.
- Online [Fact]: embed the query and match it to triples (top-k). An LLM "recognition memory" step filters irrelevant triples. Phrase seeds are weighted by the scores of the triples they came from; all passage nodes are seeds weighted by embedding similarity times a balance factor. If no triples survive, fall back to dense retrieval. Run PPR and rank passages by score.
- Models: Llama-3.3-70B and NV-Embed-v2.
- F1 [Fact]: average 59.8 against NV-Embed-v2 57.0, HippoRAG 53.1, GraphRAG 49.6, RAPTOR 48.8, LightRAG 6.6. NQ 63.3 against 61.9; MuSiQue 48.6 against 45.7; 2Wiki 71.0 against 61.5.
Builder lesson: [Inference] the best published recipe for multi-hop questions over *unstructured* text that stays safe on simple questions. Its cost is one OpenIE pass at indexing and one filter call per query.

**KAG (Ant Group).** Liang et al., arXiv:2409.13731 (Sep 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13731), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13731)). [Fact]
- Representation (LLMFriSPG) layers schema-constrained knowledge, schema-free graph information and raw chunks.
- *Mutual indexing* links graph elements to source chunks.
- A logical-form solver breaks questions into operators (Retrieval, Sort, Math, Deduce) with reflection rounds and a global memory.
- Knowledge alignment uses synonym and hypernym relations.
- Results: the abstract reports relative F1 gains of 19.6% on 2WikiMultiHopQA and 33.5% on HotpotQA over prior RAG; the HTML introduction lists the figures in a different order, so the per-dataset attribution is inconsistent. Deployed for Ant's e-government and e-health Q&A ("significant improvement in professionalism", no figures in the parts I read). Open source via OpenSPG.
Builder lesson: [Inference] the closest academic match to the founder's design. A schema-constrained company model sits alongside a looser extracted layer, both linked to source text, and questions are planned into typed operators including **Math**. Copy the layering and the operator vocabulary. The KAG-Model fine-tuning component does not apply.

**G-Retriever, Think-on-Graph, GraphReader.**
- G-Retriever: He et al., arXiv:2402.07630 (2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07630)). [Fact] Frames subgraph retrieval as Prize-Collecting Steiner Tree optimisation to pick a compact, connected, relevant subgraph; the LLM is fine-tuned with soft prompts (needs training). Copy only the idea: [Inference] when handing an agent a neighbourhood of records, return a *connected* minimal subgraph linking the relevant nodes, not a bag of top-k nodes.
- Think-on-Graph: Sun et al., arXiv:2307.07697 (v6 Mar 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07697)). [Fact] Training-free. The LLM agent runs iterative beam search over KG entities and relations and returns the best reasoning path; state of the art on 6 of 9 datasets at the time. [Inference] This is the template for agent traversal of a typed company graph through tools (`get_neighbors(object, link_type)`), with beam width and depth limits as cost controls.
- GraphReader: Li et al., arXiv:2406.14550 (v2 Nov 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14550)). [Fact] Turns long text into a graph; an agent plans, calls functions to read nodes and neighbours from coarse to fine, and records notes until it has enough. With a 4k context it beat GPT-4-128k on LV-Eval from 16k to 256k tokens.

**When graph retrieval helps and when it doesn't.**
- Han et al., "RAG vs. GraphRAG: A Systematic Evaluation and Key Insights", arXiv:2502.11371 (v3 Mar 2026) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). [Fact]
  - NQ F1: RAG 64.8, GraphRAG-Local 63.0, HippoRAG 2 61.0, KG-GraphRAG (triples only) 34.3.
  - HotpotQA: HippoRAG 2 63.0, RAG 60.0.
  - MultiHop-RAG accuracy: HippoRAG 2 70.3, RAG 67.0. RAG still leads on inference and null queries; graph methods lead on temporal queries (about 50% against 30.7%).
  - Only about 65% of answer entities appear in the LLM-built KG.
  - Hybrids: *Selection* (route fact questions to RAG and reasoning questions to GraphRAG) +1.1%; *Integration* (run both and concatenate contexts) +6.4%, at roughly double the cost.
  - Judge position bias as in Key finding 14.
- Xiang et al., "When to use Graphs in RAG" (GraphRAG-Bench), arXiv:2506.05690 (v3 Feb 2026) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690)). [Fact]
  - Fact retrieval: RAG with rerank matches or beats graphs (60.9 against 60.1).
  - Complex reasoning: HippoRAG 2 +10.5 points. Contextual summarisation: MS-GraphRAG local +13.1.
  - Graph quality: HippoRAG 2's graph is the densest (average degree 8.75).
  - Guidelines: retrieve precisely, prefer graph quality to graph size, bound context growth, use simple RAG for simple lookups.
  - The paper's text and its token-cost table disagree for MS-GraphRAG global (about 40k against about 331k).
Builder lesson: [Inference] a graph built by an LLM *from text* is lossy and expensive. A graph built *from the company's own structured records* (foreign keys, IDs) is exact and almost free. The product's main graph should be the latter, with text-derived graphs only as a secondary index.

### 6. Question answering over structured data (text-to-SQL, semantic layers, text-to-Cypher/SPARQL)

**Spider (2018).** Yu et al., arXiv:1809.08887 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08887)). [Fact] 10,181 questions, 5,693 SQL queries, 200 databases, 138 domains. Train and test databases are disjoint, testing generalisation to unseen schemas. The best baseline then had 12.4% exact match. Now saturated: 91.2% for o1-preview per Spider 2.0.

**BIRD (2023).** Li et al., arXiv:2305.03111 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03111)). [Fact] 12,751 question–SQL pairs, 95 databases totalling 33.4 GB, 37 domains; adds "external knowledge" evidence and dirty values. ChatGPT scored 40.08% execution accuracy against 92.96% for humans. Leaderboard as of November 2026: AskData (AT&T) 83.40, Agentar-Scale-SQL (Ant Group, Sep 2025) 81.67. New variants include BIRD-Interact (ICLR 2026 oral, conversational and agentic), LiveSQLBench-Large (480 tasks, about 54 tables per database) and BIRD-Critic ([leaderboard](https://bird-bench.github.io/)).

**Spider 2.0 (2024/2025).** Lei et al., arXiv:2411.07763, ICLR 2025 Oral ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763)). [Fact] 632 real enterprise workflow tasks on BigQuery, Snowflake and local databases, many with over 1,000 columns; o1-preview agent 21.3%. Leaderboard as of November 2026 ([site](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)): Snow 96.70 (Genloop Sentinel Agent v2 Pro), Lite 76.23, DBT 65.6. Entries carry no dates. [Inference] The near-saturation of Spider 2.0-Snow by heavily engineered agents shows the ceiling is reachable with scaffolding. Lite and DBT show that multi-dialect work and transformation pipelines remain hard.

**Sequeda, Allemang, Jacob 2023: the knowledge-graph benchmark.** arXiv:2311.07509 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07509)). [Fact]
- Data: 43 questions on a 13-table subset of the OMG Property & Casualty insurance model (199 tables in full).
- Quadrants by question complexity (reporting against KPI/metrics) and schema complexity (4 or fewer denormalised tables against more than 4 normalised).
- Setup: GPT-4 zero-shot, temperature 0.3. The SQL prompt gets the DDL; the SPARQL prompt gets the OWL ontology, with data accessed virtually through 1-to-1 R2RML mappings.
- Scoring: Spider-style execution accuracy averaged over runs (AOEA).
- Results: overall 16.7% (SQL) against 54.2% (KG). Low/low quadrant 25.5 against 71.1; high-schema quadrants 0% against 35.7–38.5%.
- Limits: small manual data, no date filters, GPT-4 only, authors employed by data.world.
Follow-up: Allemang & Sequeda, "Increasing the LLM Accuracy for Question Answering: Ontologies to the Rescue!", arXiv:2405.11706 (May 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706)). [Fact] **OBQC** checks the generated SPARQL against ontology semantics (for example, does a property's domain and range fit how the query uses it) and produces error explanations; **LLM repair** regenerates the query from them. Accuracy 72%, plus 8% "I don't know", error 20%.
Independent rerun: dbt Labs 2026 ([blog](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026)). [Vendor claim]
- Setup: ACME insurance, 15 tables, 11 questions × 20 runs.
- Raw schema: text-to-SQL 32.7% (GPT-4, 2023) rose to 64.5% (2026 models); Semantic Layer 60.5% to 72.7%.
- Modelled data: Semantic Layer 98.2–100%, text-to-SQL 84.1–90.0%.
- Within scope, the Semantic Layer hit 100%. Questions needing too many entity hops for MetricFlow were unanswerable until modelling was added.
- Sonnet 4.6 beat Opus 4.6 here; higher reasoning effort mostly added latency.
- Caveat: the full schema was in context, which is unrealistic at scale.

**Semantic-layer document study (2026).** Rumiantsau & Fokeev, arXiv:2604.25149 (Apr 2026) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)). [Fact] 100 questions over Contoso Retail in ClickHouse; Claude Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4. Schema only: 45.5–50.5%. Schema plus a 4 KB markdown file of measures, conventions and disambiguation rules: 67.7–68.7%. Every model gained 17–23 points (p < 0.01), and models did not differ significantly within each condition. Authors' reading: business semantics change *what the model is asked to do*. Builder lesson: [Inference] even before a full semantic layer exists, a short curated "metric and conventions card" in context is worth about 20 points.

**Industrial reports (2025–2026).**
- LinkedIn SQL Bot: Chen et al., "Text-to-SQL for Enterprise Data Analytics", arXiv:2507.14372, KDD 2025 workshop ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14372v1)). [Fact]
  - Knowledge graph built from DataHub metadata, 3 months of query logs (ICA soft-clustering of user-table access; Trino EXPLAIN plans give table popularity and common joins), example queries from code and wikis filtered by recency, execution count and certification, plus user-taught domain knowledge.
  - Query Writer: retrieve, rank, write, fix. A Researcher agent hunts for missing tables; queries are validated with EXPLAIN; an intent classifier routes between agents.
  - Results on 133 internal questions: 53% rated 4–5 and 77% rated at least 3; table recall 78%, column recall 56%; compile success 96%. Schema-only baseline: 9% rated 4 or higher.
  - Filter-value errors were the top failure (24%). Over 300 weekly active users.
- Snowflake Cortex Analyst, "Agentic Semantic Model Improvement" ([engineering blog](https://www.snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/agentic-semantic-model-text-to-sql)). [Vendor claim] Agents (creation, relationships, editor, custom instructions, evaluator) refine the YAML semantic model against BIRD question–SQL pairs. On four BIRD databases, average accuracy rose from 57% (vanilla Claude 3.5 Sonnet) to 78%. The page shows no date. Snowflake's separate BI-accuracy post would not load (client error) [Not opened].

**Text-to-Cypher and SPARQL.**
- Ozsoy et al. (Neo4j), "Text2Cypher: Bridging Natural Language and Graph Databases", arXiv:2412.10064 (Dec 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10064)). [Fact] 44,387 instances; fine-tuning gave significant gains (numbers not in the abstract).
- TEXT2SPARQL'25 at ESWC 2025 ([CEUR preface](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4094/preface.pdf)). [Fact] DBpedia and a 50-question corporate KG (eccenca). Best corporate score 0.447 (F1/nDCG); best overall 0.426. Fine-tuned small models beat larger pretrained ones, and some systems only worked once missing prefixes were added.
[Inference] Generating graph queries is *less* mature than text-to-SQL. Don't make Cypher or SPARQL generation the main path to numbers. Give the agent typed tools or a semantic-layer API instead.

### 7. Entity resolution

**Plain English.** "ACME Sdn Bhd" in the CRM, "Acme Sdn. Bhd." in the accounting system and "ACME" on an invoice PDF are the same supplier. The company model needs one object with three source records linked to it.

**Fellegi–Sunter (1969).** I. P. Fellegi & A. B. Sunter, "A Theory for Record Linkage", *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 64(328):1183–1210, 1969 (copies via [Cornell](https://cs.cornell.edu/~shmat/courses/cs6434/fellegi-sunter.pdf) and [UW](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse590q/04au/papers/Felligi69.pdf)); citation matched to the search listing, PDF not opened. As implemented in Splink ([theory page](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html)) [Fact]:
- λ is the prior probability that a pair matches.
- For each compared field, *m* = P(field agrees | match) and *u* = P(field agrees | non-match).
- Match weight = log2(λ/(1−λ)) + Σ log2(m_i/u_i), assuming independence; probability = 2^M/(1+2^M).
- Blocking rules restrict which pairs are compared: n(n−1)/2 is about 500 billion pairs for 1M records, and the pair count drives run time ([Splink blocking](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/blocking/blocking_rules.html)).
- Splink estimates m and u with EM and random sampling, as I understand it; the pages I opened point to a separate training guide [Unverified].

**Ditto.** Li et al., "Deep Entity Matching with Pre-Trained Language Models", PVLDB 14 (DOI 10.14778/3421424.3421431), arXiv:2004.00584 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00584)). [Fact] Treats matching as sequence-pair classification with fine-tuned BERT/RoBERTa, plus domain-knowledge highlighting, summarisation of long values and hard-example augmentation. Up to 29% F1 over the prior state of the art; matches it with half the labels; 96.5% F1 on company tables of 789K × 412K records. Needs training, so for the product it would be a self-hosted small model [Inference].

**LLM matching.**
- Peeters, Steiner, Bizer, "Entity Matching using Large Language Models", arXiv:2310.11244 (v4 Oct 2024) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244)). [Fact] With zero or few examples, the best LLMs match PLMs fine-tuned on thousands of examples and are more robust to unseen entities. No single prompt is best everywhere. GPT-4 can explain its decisions and cluster its own errors.
- Wang et al., "Match, Compare, or Select?", arXiv:2405.16884 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16884)). [Fact] *Selecting* the match from a candidate list beats pairwise yes/no matching. The ComEM framework combines strategies and models for lower cost.
Builder lesson: [Inference] a cascade. Deterministic keys first (tax ID, registration number, email), then Fellegi–Sunter scoring with blocking, auto-accepting high weights. For the grey zone, ask an LLM to *select* the best candidate from the top-k with a stated reason. Show low-confidence merges to a human, and keep every merge reversible with provenance.

### 8. Parsing business documents

**LayoutLMv3.** arXiv:2204.08387 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08387)). [Fact] A multimodal transformer using linear patch embeddings (no CNN), pre-trained with masked language modelling, masked image modelling and word-patch alignment on about 11M IIT-CDIP pages. FUNSD F1 92.08, CORD F1 97.46, DocVQA ANLS 83.37 (large). Authors (Huang, Lv, Cui, Lu, Wei, Microsoft) and venue (ACM Multimedia 2022) are from memory [Unverified on page]. Needs fine-tuning per document type.

**DocILE.** Šimsa et al., arXiv:2302.05658 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)). [Fact] 6.7k annotated business documents (invoices and similar) with 55 field classes, 100k synthetic and about 1M unlabelled. Tasks: key information localisation and extraction (KILE) and line item recognition (LIR). Baselines: RoBERTa, LayoutLMv3, Table Transformer.

**Docling.** Auer et al. (IBM), arXiv:2408.09869 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09869)). [Fact] MIT-licensed PDF converter using a DocLayNet-trained layout model and TableFormer for table structure; runs on commodity hardware.

**OmniDocBench.** Ouyang et al., arXiv:2412.07626 (v2 Mar 2025) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07626)). [Fact] 981 pages, 9 document types. Metrics: normalised edit distance, TEDS for tables, CDM for formulas, reading order. Overall English edit distance (lower is better): MinerU 0.150, Mathpix 0.191, GPT-4o 0.233, Qwen2-VL-72B 0.252, Marker 0.336. Table TEDS (English): MinerU 78.6, Qwen2-VL 76.8, GPT-4o 72.0. Pipelines win on common layouts such as financial reports; VLMs handle unusual layouts and blurry scans better and struggle with dense pages.

**olmOCR.** Poznanski et al. (AI2), arXiv:2502.18443 (v3 Jul 2025) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18443)). [Fact] A fine-tuned 7B VLM; about $176 per million pages against over $6,240 for GPT-4o; beats GPT-4o, Gemini Flash 2 and Qwen-2.5-VL on olmOCR-Bench (1,400 PDFs).

**Invoices: native image against parsed text.** Berghaus et al. (Fraunhofer IAIS), arXiv:2509.04469 (Aug 2025) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04469v1)). [Fact] Test sets: 500 synthetic invoices, 1,000 SROIE receipts, 350 scanned invoices; zero-shot GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 and Gemma 3. Field accuracy from native images against Docling markdown: receipts 87.46% against 47.00% at best; scanned invoices 92.71% against 64.03%; clean invoices 96.50% against 85.14%. IBANs were consistently the weakest field. Exception: tiny models (Gemma 3 4B) did better from markdown on clean invoices.

**UniKIE-Bench.** Ji et al. (Northeastern, Tsinghua, Alibaba), arXiv:2602.07038 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07038)); date not stated on the page, around early 2026 [Unverified date]. [Fact] 6,133 documents; schema-guided single-pass extraction scored by field exact-match F1. Gemini-3-Pro 82.37 (constrained) and 81.65 (open); best open model Qwen3-VL-8B 79.12 and 67.36. The commercial scenario scored about 45–54 for all models. Failure types: digit misreads, layout misattribution, wrong-field assignment, and hallucinated values for missing fields.
Builder lesson: [Inference] use a layout pipeline (Docling or MinerU) for long text documents feeding retrieval. For field extraction from invoices and receipts, send page images to a frontier VLM with a typed schema. Validate every extracted value with arithmetic checks (line items sum to the subtotal, tax rate × base = tax), format checks (IBAN or bank-account checksums) and existence checks (the supplier resolves to a known object). Route failures to human review.

### 9. Table and numeric reasoning

- **TAT-QA.** Zhu et al., arXiv:2105.07624, 2021 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07624)). [Fact] Hybrid table plus text QA from real financial reports. The TAGOP model tags the relevant cells and spans, then applies symbolic aggregation operators: 58.0% F1 against 90.8% for experts. The dataset size of about 16.6k questions is from memory [Unverified].
- **FinQA.** Chen et al., arXiv:2109.00122, 2021 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00122)). [Fact] Multi-step numeric reasoning over financial reports, annotated with gold reasoning programs; pre-trained models fall far short of experts. The size of about 8.3k QA pairs and the DSL of add/subtract/multiply/divide/greater/exp plus table aggregation are from memory [Unverified].
- **Program of Thoughts.** Chen, Ma, Wang, Cohen, arXiv:2211.12588, TMLR 2023 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588)). [Fact] The model writes a program and an interpreter executes it; about 12% average gain over chain-of-thought on math and financial QA (FinQA, ConvFinQA, TAT-QA).
- **Chain-of-Table.** Wang et al. (Google), arXiv:2401.04398 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04398)). [Fact] The LLM repeatedly picks a table operation (add column, select rows, group, sort) and applies it; the evolving table records the reasoning. State of the art on WikiTQ, FeTaQA and TabFact at publication.
- **FinanceBench.** Islam et al. (Patronus AI), arXiv:2311.11944 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)). [Fact] 10,231 questions on public-company filings; on a 150-case sample, GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval was wrong or refused on 81%. Long context helps but is impractical for large filings.
Builder lesson: [Inference] numbers should come from either (i) the semantic layer or query engine, or (ii) values extracted from documents and then computed in a sandbox (Python or SQL over a temporary table). The LLM never adds numbers in its head. Every displayed number should carry a trace: the query or program and the source rows or cells.

## What to copy

1. **Canonical declarative company model, compiled to targets.** Store object types, properties, link types, metric definitions and rules in one versioned declarative store. Generate SQL views, agent tool schemas, validation shapes and prompt "cards" from it. Why: Gruber's portability argument, and Guarino's ontology-driven systems at run time. Sources: [Gruber 1993](https://tomgruber.org/writing/ontolingua-kaj-1993/), [Guarino 1998](https://www.loa.istc.cnr.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/FOIS98.pdf).
2. **Keep the expressivity OWL 2 QL-like so queries compile to SQL over the customer's data in place.** Allow class hierarchy, typed links and domain/range. Avoid transitive closure and cardinality inference in the query path. Source: [OWL 2 Profiles](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/).
3. **Business rules as SHACL-style shapes.** For each object type: required properties (`minCount`), datatypes, allowed values (`in`), link targets (`class`), with a severity of info, warning or violation. Validation returns per-record reports (focus node, path, value, message). Use the same shapes to validate document extractions and agent write actions. Source: [SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/).
4. **Model roles as relationships, not subclasses (OntoClean lint).** Customer and Supplier are roles of an Organisation. Reject an LLM-proposed subclass of an anti-rigid type over a rigid one. Source: [OntoClean, CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/evaluating-ontological-decisions-with-ontoclean).
5. **Answer numeric questions through a metric/semantic layer API, not free SQL, and refuse out-of-scope questions.** The agent picks metric, dimensions, filters and time grain; a deterministic compiler writes the SQL. Out of scope returns a typed error. Sources: [dbt 2026](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026), [Sequeda 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07509).
6. **Fallback text-to-SQL gets an ontology-based query check plus a repair loop.** Before execution, check that every table and column exists, joins follow declared links, aggregations apply only to measures and filters use valid values. On failure, feed the explanation back for at most N repairs, then answer "I don't know". Sources: [OBQC, 2405.11706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706), [LinkedIn EXPLAIN validation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14372v1).
7. **Always include a curated "conventions card" (about 4 KB) for each data domain in context.** It holds measure definitions, fiscal calendar, currency, "active customer" definition and disambiguation rules. Source: [2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149).
8. **Mine query logs and example queries into the model.** Table popularity and common joins from EXPLAIN plans; certified example queries retrieved as few-shot examples; table clusters per team. These drove most of LinkedIn's gains. Source: [2507.14372](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14372v1).
9. **Onboarding generates the model from the schema with LLM drafts plus human confirmation.** Table by table along foreign keys, emitting provenance-tagged *deltas* reviewed by a judge LLM and then a human (RIGOR). Mapping split into constrained sub-tasks (2609.13776). Implicit FK discovery by name, type, value overlap and cardinality (Yonyou). Relationships are never accepted from the LLM alone. Sources: [RIGOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01232v1), [self-demonstrations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13776), [Yonyou](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00029v1), [LLMs4OL](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16648).
10. **Competency questions are the onboarding interface and the regression suite.** Collect 30–100 real questions per customer, generate the model question by question, and keep the expected answers as execution-accuracy tests run on every model change. Sources: [Ontology Generation 2503.05388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05388v1), [Spider execution accuracy as used in 2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07509).
11. **Document retrieval baseline: structural chunks, contextual prefix, BM25 plus dense, rerank 150 down to 20.** Source: [Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval), [element chunking 2402.05131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05131), [BEIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663).
12. **Small corpora go to long context with caching; otherwise Self-Route.** If a scoped document set is under about 200k tokens, put it all in context. Otherwise RAG first, with "unanswerable" as an allowed answer, then retry with expanded context. Sources: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval), [Self-Route 2407.16833](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16833).
13. **Retrieval sufficiency gate and claim-support check.** An LLM judges whether the evidence is enough (CRAG's correct, ambiguous or incorrect); if not, query another source or abstain. After generation, check each claim is fully supported by a cited chunk (Self-RAG's IsSup) and strip or flag unsupported claims. Sources: [CRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15884), [Self-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11511).
14. **Typed traversal tools for multi-hop over records (Think-on-Graph pattern).** Expose `find_objects(type, filter)`, `get_links(object_id, link_type, direction)` and `get_object(id, properties)` with beam and depth limits. Return connected minimal subgraphs (G-Retriever's idea). Sources: [ToG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07697), [G-Retriever](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07630).
15. **Mutual indexing between objects and text.** Every extracted fact or chunk links to the object IDs it mentions, and every object links back to its supporting chunks, so answers cite both. Source: [KAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13731).
16. **For multi-hop over unstructured text, use HippoRAG 2-style PPR, not GraphRAG or LightRAG.** OpenIE triples, then phrase plus passage nodes, synonym edges above a similarity threshold, query-to-triple matching, an LLM filter, and PPR seeded by phrases and passages with fallback to dense retrieval. Sources: [HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802), [GraphRAG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690).
17. **Global "what are the themes" questions over large corpora: LazyGraphRAG-style deferred work.** Cheap noun-phrase co-occurrence communities at index time, LLM relevance tests under a fixed budget at query time. Source: [LazyGraphRAG](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/).
18. **Entity resolution cascade.** Exact keys, then Fellegi–Sunter weights with blocking (Splink), then LLM *selection* among the top-k candidates for the grey zone, then human review. Merges are reversible with provenance. Sources: [Splink](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html), [Peeters 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244), [Match/Compare/Select](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16884).
19. **Invoice and receipt extraction: page image to frontier VLM with a typed schema, plus arithmetic, checksum and existence validators.** Long reports go through a layout parser for retrieval. Sources: [2509.04469](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04469v1), [UniKIE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07038), [OmniDocBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07626).
20. **All arithmetic in executed code with a provenance trace.** PoT/PAL in a sandbox over values pulled from the semantic layer or from extractions. Sources: [PoT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588), [FinanceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944).
21. **Evaluation hygiene.** Use execution accuracy for numbers and exact or F1 scores for facts. If you use pairwise LLM judges, run both orders and several judges. Source: [2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371).

### Recommendation: what a small product should build first

**(a) Questions about numbers.** Build first: metric definitions in the company model, a deterministic metric-query compiler (metric × dimensions × filters × grain → SQL), a conventions card in context, and a typed refusal for out-of-scope questions (items 5 and 7). Second: guarded text-to-SQL for exploration with validation against the model, EXPLAIN dry-run and a bounded repair loop (item 6), plus certified example queries mined from query logs (item 8). All derived arithmetic runs in code (item 20). Evidence: SQL alone scored 16.7% against 54.2% over a KG and 72% with checks (Sequeda/Allemang); 98–100% for the semantic layer against 84–90% for text-to-SQL (dbt 2026); +17–23 points from a 4 KB semantics document (2604.25149); 9% against 48% with metadata at LinkedIn; 0% text-to-SQL on high-schema questions (2311.07509). Skip first: Cypher or SPARQL generation (TEXT2SPARQL best corporate score 0.447) and fine-tuned SQL models (third-party models only).

**(b) Answering from documents.** Build first: layout-aware parsing (Docling or MinerU) into structural chunks; contextual prefixes; hybrid BM25 plus dense retrieval with a reranker (150 down to 20); whole-scope long context when the scope is under about 200k tokens; Self-Route fallback; a sufficiency gate and claim-support check; citations to chunk and page (items 11–13). For field-bearing documents (invoices, POs, receipts), VLM extraction from images into the company model's typed schema with validators (item 19), so later questions about those documents become questions about numbers (path a). Evidence: retrieval failure 5.7% down to 1.9% (Contextual Retrieval); semantic chunking not worth its cost (2410.13070); Self-Route matches long context at 39–65% lower cost; native image 87.5% against 47.0% on receipts (2509.04469); CRAG gains of +7 to +37 points. Skip first: RAPTOR (below dense retrieval on average in HippoRAG 2's tests), Self-RAG training, ColBERT infrastructure.

**(c) Multi-hop questions across records.** Build first: the *record graph from structured data* (foreign keys and resolved entities as typed links), exposed to agents through typed traversal tools with depth and beam limits (item 14), with entity resolution (item 18) and mutual indexing to documents (item 15). This avoids the main weakness of LLM-built graphs, in which only about 65% of answer entities make it in (2502.11371). Second, only if customers ask multi-hop questions about *unstructured* text: a HippoRAG 2-style PPR index (item 16), the one graph method that beat strong dense retrieval on average (59.8 against 57.0 F1) without losing on simple questions, at about 1k tokens per query. Evidence against the alternatives: plain RAG beats GraphRAG on single-hop questions; LightRAG scored 6.6 average F1 and about 100k tokens per query in independent tests; GraphRAG global used about 331k tokens per query (GraphRAG-Bench). LazyGraphRAG is a later add-on for corpus-wide summary questions (item 17).

## What cannot or should not be copied

- **Anything that requires training the answering model.** Self-RAG (trained generator and critic), G-Retriever (soft-prompt tuning), the KAG-Model, Yonyou's LOM, fine-tuned Text2Cypher and SPARQL models, Ditto. This conflicts with the founder's "third-party models only" principle. Copy their *checks and structures*, not their weights. Exception: [Inference] a small self-hosted matcher or reranker is not "model training" of the answering LLM, but it is still a maintenance burden for a solo founder.
- **GraphRAG global search as a default.** About 331k tokens per query in GraphRAG-Bench; it is meant for corpus-wide sensemaking, and its reported wins rest on order-biased LLM judging ([2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)).
- **LightRAG's headline numbers.** They conflict sharply with two independent benchmarks. Don't adopt it on the strength of its own paper.
- **Benchmark leaderboard systems (Spider 2.0-Snow at about 97, BIRD at about 83).** These are heavily engineered agents tuned to the benchmark, often closed and undocumented. Their scores do not carry over to a customer's messy schema. Spider 2.0-Lite and DBT are far lower.
- **Fully automatic ontology construction.** Relation extraction F1 is very low (0.078 in LLMs4OL 2024), and drafts contain recurring mistakes. Human confirmation is required, especially for links and metric definitions.
- **Full OWL DL reasoning in the query path.** Expressive OWL gives up the guaranteed SQL rewriting of OWL 2 QL, and an open-world semantics confuses users who expect "missing means no". Use SHACL-style closed-world rules.
- **The exact Sequeda accuracy figures as a promise.** 43 questions, a 13-table subset, GPT-4 in 2023, authors employed by data.world; dbt is a vendor; Snowflake is a vendor. The *direction* is consistent across sources; the magnitudes are not guarantees. [Inference]
