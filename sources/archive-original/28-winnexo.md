# WinNexo (Lingyang, Alibaba Cloud): product flow and how it operates

Scope note (written 21 November 2026). This file reads WinNexo's public English help centre at `robject.lydaas.com/help/en/` and records the product flow: what an administrator sets up, what a user does, and what each module is. It was written by the lead researcher, not a sub-agent. Seventeen pages were read; the pages not read are listed at the end. Everything marked Fact is documented product behaviour from the vendor's own help centre, which is good evidence of what the product does but not of how well it does it. No customer names, prices or usage figures appear in the pages read.

## Key findings

1. **WinNexo is the closest published product to the founder's plan.** It is described as "an AI-powered data work companion" from Alibaba Cloud Lingyang that lets a team build role-level "Digital Workers" on top of an ontology, business rules, skills and knowledge bases ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/overview)).
2. **It has two on-ramps, and the first needs no data modelling.** The quickstart is four steps: create a position, add a knowledge base by uploading files, chat, schedule a task. The page says about 10 minutes ([Vendor claim](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/quickstart)). The ontology comes later, in "Advanced Usage" ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced)).
3. **The unit of deployment is the position, not the agent.** A position bundles object types, business logic, analysis views, skills and knowledge bases, plus the users allowed to use it. The docs advise one business function per position because overloading reduces accuracy ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced)).
4. **The ontology is generated by AI from a scenario description and selected tables, then reviewed by a person.** The reviewer sees added, modified and deleted definitions, corrects them by form or in a YAML editing mode, and publishes ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced)).
5. **Business logic has four types: process, logic, rule and indicator (metric).** Each is a natural-language description tied to objects, and can be created by a guided form or pasted in bulk as YAML ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/business-logic/)).
6. **Model and data mapping are separate, and nothing is live until published.** There is one shared draft per ontology; saving does not apply changes; "Validate and Publish" does ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/edit-yaml)).
7. **Skills use the same file format as Anthropic's skills.** A custom skill is a `.zip` or `.md` whose root holds `SKILL.md` with name and description in YAML. Skills can also be created by describing them in chat ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced)).
8. **It lives inside chat tools.** Each agent maps to one bot in DingTalk, Feishu or WeCom; users pair with a six-digit code; scheduled results are pushed into chats ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/support/faq)).
9. **It ships weekly.** The changelog shows releases on 3, 10, 17 and 24 September, 15 and 22 October, and 2, 5, 12 and 19 November 2026 ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
10. **Relationship to AgentOne.** The FAQ lists outbound addresses for regions named "agentone-Zhangjiakou" and "agentone-Beijing" beside Shanghai and Hangzhou ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/support/faq)). [Inference] WinNexo runs on, or is a product line of, the AgentOne platform. The help centre pages read do not state the relationship.

## Breakdown

### 1. What it is

Plain English: a company describes its business objects and rules once. It then creates "positions" such as Sales Assistant. Each position becomes an AI worker that staff talk to in a web workspace or in their chat tool, and that can run tasks on a schedule.

- Three layers: an ontology layer (objects, attributes, relationships, calculation rules), a role layer (Digital Workers) and an action layer (skills, knowledge, chat channels) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/overview)).
- Five modules: Ontology, Skill Center (60+ built-in skills), Knowledge Center, Digital Workers, and AI Workspace ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/overview)).
- Target users: data analysts, financial analysts and sales operations staff use the workers; platform administrators, data architects and AI application engineers build them. The overview says a team can be running within a week and an individual in about 10 minutes ([Vendor claim](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/overview)).
- The overview positions it against general chatbots on five points: grounding in the enterprise ontology, rule-based answers, executable skills, role-separated workers and multi-channel collaboration ([Vendor claim](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/overview)).

### 2. The simple flow (no data model)

Source for this section: [Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/quickstart).

| Step | Where | What the person does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Create a position | Admin Console > Positions > Create Position | Enter a name (letters, numbers, underscores, hyphens), a display name, an avatar, and one or two sentences on responsibilities |
| 2 Add a knowledge base | Admin Console > Knowledge Bases > New Knowledge Base | Upload files (PDF, Word, Excel, PPT, images, audio, video, up to 2 GB each), link DingTalk or Feishu documents for automatic sync, add meeting recordings, or write notes. Link the knowledge base to positions and set visibility. Wait for status Waiting > Processing > Success |
| 3 Chat | Workspace | Pick the Digital Worker, ask in natural language. `@` references knowledge; `/` or shortcut buttons invoke skills. Reasoning steps are shown |
| 4 Schedule a task | Automated Tasks > Add Task | Describe the task in natural language, pick skills if needed. Results appear under execution records and "My Outputs", and can be sent to a chat group |

Recommended practice on the same page: one Digital Worker per position; start with basic knowledge and skills and refine from real conversations; review answer quality every two weeks.

### 3. The full flow (with the ontology)

Source for this section: [Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced).

| Step | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Accounts and data sources | Platform Management > User Management; Data Source Configuration | Organise users into groups by position (the page names platform administrators, frontline business users and managers of specific positions). Connect databases. The page warns that inconsistent field names, keys and comments in physical tables lead the AI to produce an unusable model |
| 2 Build an ontology | Ontology > AI Generation | Create an ontology space, describe the business scenario and goals, select source tables, click Generate. The AI extracts entities, creates object types and business logic, identifies relationships and configures table mappings. The person reviews added, modified and deleted definitions and corrects them by hand or in YAML editing mode |
| 3 Create skills | Skill Center | Use built-in skills (office documents, follow-up emails, meeting summaries, sales collateral, outreach strategies, lead discovery) or create custom ones: describe the skill in chat and let the model write it, or import a `.zip` or `.md` containing `SKILL.md`. Debug, save, publish |
| 4 Create a position | Positions > Create Position | Attach object types, business logic, analysis views, a skill scope and knowledge bases. Assign users and administrators |
| 5 Use the workspace | Workspace | Users sign in and pick the worker for their position |

Acceptance check before rollout, from the same page: ask questions the worker should answer and confirm it uses the ontology and knowledge base; ask questions outside its permissions and confirm they are refused; run one or two custom skills and check the output.

### 4. The ontology module

- An ontology space has four functions: Ontology Model, Data Mapping, Ontology Optimization, and Validate and Publish. The stated order of work is to define business meaning first, associate data second, then validate and publish ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/)).
- Seven definition types: object types, relationships, business processes, logic, rules, metrics and analysis views. Analysis views provide reusable dimensions and measures. There is a graph view and a list view ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/)).
- Two construction methods: AI-assisted (from business materials or data tables, producing candidate definitions that a person reviews and adopts) and manual ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/create/)).
- Editing: a form per element with a "View YAML" switch, or "Manual Construction Mode" for the whole model in form or YAML mode. There is one shared "Draft Pending Publication" per ontology. The page states that saving a model does not mean its data is ready to query ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/edit-yaml)).
- Cross-table attributes: an object attribute can come from another physical table by selecting the table, the value field and a join key that matches the object's `_id`. Link attributes attach files from object storage or from a knowledge base selected by tags with AND or OR matching ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/edit-yaml)).
- Not published on the pages read: the YAML schema itself. The page refers to "Examples and Specifications" inside the product.

### 5. Business logic

| Type | Key | What it is | Examples given |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business process | `process` | A workflow with start and end states and key stages | Product development, project acceptance, contract signing |
| Logic | `logic` | Criteria and calculations for filtering and deriving values | VIP customers, online-payment contracts, high-risk projects |
| Rule | `rule` | Constraints and validation checks | A risk needs a resolution before it can be closed; milestone date validation |
| Metric | `indicator` | A key measurement and its formula | GAAP completion rate, project risk count, milestone completion rate |

Source: [Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/business-logic/).

- Creation: a guided form (name, display name, associated object, description of the core logic) or one YAML block holding several entries, ingested one at a time with failures reported individually. A "Load Template" button pre-fills a sample ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/business-logic/create)).
- Nesting: indicators can link to processes and rules can link to logic. The page's example: the indicator "GAAP completion rate" is associated with Project and linked to the "project acceptance" process; the AI counts projects whose acceptance node is completed and divides by the total ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/business-logic/create)).
- [Inference] The pages read describe these definitions as descriptions the AI applies. They do not say whether a metric is compiled to a fixed query or interpreted by the model each time. This matters: the founder's plan compiles metrics so the same question always gives the same number. Whether WinNexo does the same is not established by these pages.

### 6. Permissions

- Four ontology permission types: definition (create, view, edit objects, logic and views), maintenance (edit basic information, publish, delete), property value modification (write to object data) and property value viewing (see original values). Publishing does not grant editing, and editing definitions does not grant changing business data ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/admin/ontology/model/permissions)).
- Data permissions apply to both table and graph queries, and external user attributes with several values can be synced for them ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/), releases 1119 and 1112).
- A position limits what its worker can touch: object types, logic, views, skills and knowledge bases ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/getting-started/advanced)).

### 7. Automated tasks, events and workflows

- A task has an execution plan (manual, scheduled daily, weekly, monthly or by Cron, or triggered by an external notification), a natural-language description, and optionally locked objects, ontology elements and skills ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/user-guide/automated-tasks/)).
- One worker can run a task alone, or up to five as a team in which a lead worker splits the work ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/user-guide/automated-tasks/)).
- Each run is logged with a status (Success, Failed, Pending, Running, Terminated), outputs and logs; a failed run can be retried from the failed step. Tasks can be distributed centrally to users or groups, who run them with their own permissions, and saved as enterprise templates ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/user-guide/automated-tasks/)).
- Event Center (beta, 3 Sep 2026): triggers from opportunity stage changes, contract amounts, chat messages, mentions, negative feedback and group membership changes, with conditions in natural language and cooldown windows ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Collaboration Space workflows (beta 17 Sep, extended 2 Nov 2026): templates with steps, branches, deliverables, human approval, a run summary and an audit page ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Business Actions (15 and 22 Oct 2026): actions configured in natural language with input formats, execution conditions and batch runs, reusable in chats and tasks ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).

### 8. Knowledge

- Sources: uploaded files, linked DingTalk and Feishu documents, WeCom online documents, meeting recordings and summaries, group chat messages and attachments, WeChat Official Account articles, and existing external knowledge bases through search, directory and detail endpoints ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Personal, space and enterprise knowledge bases share one set of ingestion sources (24 Sep 2026). A shared knowledge base can improve itself, with conflict detection and approval of changes (24 Sep 2026). An "LLM Wiki" builds topic graphs, overviews and topic cards (22 Oct 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Answers can show numbered sources for knowledge passages, web pages and ontology calculations (12 Nov 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Graph data: an ontology can be connected to a Neo4j graph database, read-only, for multi-hop tracing with paths and sources; a Dataphin operator builds graphs from unstructured documents (19 Nov 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).

### 9. Chat channels

| Capability | DingTalk | Ali DingTalk | Feishu | WeCom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-to-one and group chat bot | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Group messages collected into knowledge | Yes | Yes | Yes | Limited |
| Meetings collected into knowledge | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Documents collected into knowledge | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Replies in a group without being mentioned | No | No | No | No |

Source: [Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/support/im-capability-comparison).

- WeCom integration has three independent parts: account linking (who can log in, with organisation sync), mobile access (a mini app or a mobile web page) and the channel bot (chat and pushed results). One agent maps to one bot. Users pair once with a six-digit code valid for five minutes ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/support/faq)).

### 10. Testing and improvement

- Evaluation Center: business question cases, batch evaluation, AI-generated cases and suggested semantic optimisations (2 Nov 2026); a feedback dashboard combining web and DingTalk feedback (19 Nov 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Conversation Scanning: scheduled scans of conversations with custom analysis, feeding ontology optimisation suggestions (12 Nov 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- AI Troubleshooting is available to end users in chat (3 Sep 2026) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).

### 11. Models, credits and cost controls

- Models selectable by name on 5 Nov 2026: Deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017, GLM-5.3 and Kimi-K3, each with a usage multiplier, plus an Auto mode with a preference for quality, cost or neither ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Two work modes: Quick Q&A and Deep Work, with reasoning effort Low, Medium or High and "usage for this round" shown ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)).
- Credits: separate concurrency and credit quotas for individuals and spaces, spending averages and limit notifications ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/changelog/)). No prices appear on the pages read.

### 12. API and command line

- 82 endpoints under `/openapi`, with bearer tokens of three kinds: a user token (`wn_`), a deployment token (`wn_dpl_`) and an encrypted token. Groups: chat (7), skills (4), personal resources (19), knowledge base (16), scheduled tasks (8), object data (3), user and billing (4), user and permission management (15), organisation sync (4) ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/extensions/openapi)).
- Tenant and user identifiers may not be passed in request bodies; they are derived from the token. Skill runs are asynchronous: `runSkill` returns a run id and `getSkillRun` is polled ([Fact](https://robject.lydaas.com/help/en/extensions/openapi)).

## What to copy

1. **Two on-ramps.** A no-model path (create a role, drop in files, chat, schedule) that works in minutes, and a model path added later. *Why:* the user sees value before any modelling. *Source:* quickstart and advanced pages.
2. **The position as the unit.** One role = objects + logic + views + skills + knowledge + allowed users. *Why:* it is the permission boundary and the thing a layman understands. *Source:* advanced page.
3. **AI proposes, a person adopts.** Generated definitions arrive as candidates showing what was added, changed and deleted. *Source:* create and advanced pages.
4. **Model and mapping kept apart, with one shared draft and an explicit publish step.** *Source:* edit-yaml page.
5. **Four business-logic types (process, logic, rule, metric), each tied to objects and linkable to each other.** *Source:* business-logic pages.
6. **Bulk YAML import with per-entry failure reports and a "load template" button.** *Source:* business-logic create page.
7. **The three-question acceptance check:** should answer, should refuse, skill output. *Source:* advanced page.
8. **`SKILL.md` as the skill format, creatable by chat.** *Source:* advanced page.
9. **One agent, one bot; pairing by a short code; pushed results.** *Source:* FAQ.
10. **Tasks that run with the recipient's own permissions when distributed.** *Source:* automated tasks page.
11. **Numbered sources in answers, including for calculations.** *Source:* changelog 1112.
12. **An evaluation centre fed by real conversations.** *Source:* changelog 1102, 1112, 1119.

## What not to copy

- A graph database, multi-worker teams, a collaboration workspace, video generation and an 82-endpoint API. These arrived months into a funded product's life.
- Chinese chat tools only. Outside China the equivalents are WhatsApp, Slack, Teams and Lark.

## Where the founder's plan differs

- WinNexo's documentation assumes an administrator, a data architect and connected databases for the full flow, and names "a week" for an enterprise team. The founder's plan is for organisations with no such staff.
- The plan compiles official numbers deterministically. Whether WinNexo does is not established (section 5).
- The plan includes an approval queue with action grades as a core part. WinNexo documents human approval inside workflows and conditions on Business Actions; the detail pages were not read.
